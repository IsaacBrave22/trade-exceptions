    How it works

    Open exceptions. Every traded player exception on Hoops Rumors' tracker as of September 27, 2026, with amounts already used taken out.

    Real buying power. An exception's face value is the most salary it can absorb. The rules can shrink that. A team above the first apron ($209.0M) can't use an exception it created last season, and using one hard-caps it at that apron, so its buying power is capped at its room under $209.0M. Teams already hard-capped from offseason moves are limited the same way, at the first apron or the second ($221.7M). Exceptions that Hoops Rumors marks as unusable for apron reasons are listed as frozen. Payrolls are Spotrac's apron totals as of November 4, 2026.

    Chance it gets used. I pulled 120 archived versions of the Hoops Rumors tracker from 2020 to 2026 and followed every exception from creation to expiration: 317 in all. An exception counts as used if any of it was spent. Ones that vanished during an offseason were left out, since those were usually wiped when a team used cap space. The chance shown is the post-2024 usage rate for exceptions of that size, lightly smoothed because the samples are small.

    Limits. Archive snapshots are weeks apart, so a few uses could be timed wrong. A frozen exception can come back to life if a team cuts payroll below the apron. And an exception only works if there's a deal on the other end.

    Data: Hoops Rumors trade exception tracker (via the Internet Archive), Spotrac payrolls, NBA.com cap figures. 2026-27 first apron: $209.0M.Isaac Braverman
