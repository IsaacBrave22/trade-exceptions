    How this works

    Open exceptions. Every traded player exception on Hoops Rumors' tracker, with amounts already spent taken out. Last checked November 10, 2026, after the Thunder used part of their Aaron Wiggins exception on Nique Clifford.

    What it can absorb. Face value is the most salary an exception can take in. The rules can shrink that. A team above the first apron ($209.0M) can't use an exception it created last season, and using one hard-caps it at that apron, so its buying power stops at its room under $209.0M. Teams already hard-capped from offseason moves are limited the same way, at the first apron or the second ($221.7M). Exceptions Hoops Rumors marks as unusable for apron reasons are listed as frozen. Payrolls are Spotrac's apron totals as of November 4, 2026, adjusted for the Clifford trade.

    How often they get used. I pulled 120 archived versions of the Hoops Rumors tracker from 2020 to 2026 and followed every exception from creation to expiration. An exception counts as used if any of it was spent. Ones that vanished during an offseason were left out, since those were usually wiped when a team used cap space, which leaves 272. The rate shown on each exception is the share of same-size exceptions used since the aprons took effect in 2024-25.

    Limits. Archive snapshots are weeks apart, so a few uses could be timed wrong. A frozen exception can come back to life if a team cuts payroll below the apron. And an exception only works if there's a deal on the other end.
